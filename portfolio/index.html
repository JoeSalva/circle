How I work

Three war stories from shipping the thing above. Each one changed how I build.

Measure first, fix second

My app "felt fine" while making 54 queries per page. Performance bugs hide until real traffic shows up, and then your users are the load testers. Now I get counts right on day one and let the database tell me the truth.

Test in an environment that hates you

A cache line passed on my laptop and exploded on a clean machine, because delete_pattern only exists on Redis-backed caches. The fix was a capability check, not a try/except. Fallbacks are features; my settings now degrade on purpose.

Constraints are law

Serializer validation is a polite suggestion. A check-then-create like toggle has a race condition; two simultaneous requests ship a double like. UniqueConstraint in the database makes that class of bug impossible instead of unlikely.

On AI-assisted development: I use it for boilerplate, stack traces, and docs. I verify everything it produces: my test suite is where the truth gets checked, and tests don't care how confident generated code sounds.
